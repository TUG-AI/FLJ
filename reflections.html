Bob's Reflections

Some thoughts on the background resources as I work through them. These aren't meant to be authoritative—just one person's attempt to make sense of what we're reading.

Week 2: Cleansing the Temple

On Chad Bird's Video

Why Did Christ Cleanse the Temple? The Crucial Old Testament Background (24 min)

This video was recommended by an AI search, and I'd never heard of Chad Bird before. But I watched it, and I liked it. It's deep. He appears to be very scholarly and respected, and his exegesis rang true to me.

To understand John's account of Jesus cleansing the temple, Bird suggests you need to absorb two key passages: Psalm 69 and Zechariah 14. About ten minutes in, I realized I needed to pause and look up these passages—it gets dense.

The Democratization of Holiness

Bird puts together something from Zechariah 14 that I found lovely and encouraging. The High Priest's crown bore inscriptions signifying holiness—unique to their role, worn only in particular places by set-apart people. But Zechariah 14 prophesies that this holiness will extend to ordinary items: horse bells, household pots. Ordinary pots and pans in people's houses will be as holy as what's in the temple.

This connects to the tearing of the veil—holiness democratized. And that's the background for Jesus driving out the traders.

Driving Out the "Canaanites"

Bird interprets Jesus driving out the "traders" as driving out the "Canaanites"—a word that meant trader and particular kinds of exchange. As a business person, this can seem challenging, since I consider what I do holy and sacred.

But as I've thought about it: It can be good to act in business and create value in the world. But the connection between you and the Creator has no transactional cost. There's nothing to trade. No mediator needed.

We mediate between each other because of our finiteness—that's why holy, wholesome business can demonstrate God's love. But God doesn't need that. He's not limited, and in Him, we're not limited that way.

"Your money ain't good here. I'll give you the money that'll work, but you gotta exchange, and I get to set the rate. If you want to be holy, if you want to make your sacrifice, you've gotta go through me."

Jesus says: Get out of the way. There is no barrier between God the Father and you. You don't need to change your money. You don't need the goats—the ultimate Lamb is here.

Viewing tip: If it gets too dense after the first ten minutes, skip to the last couple minutes for a good summary.

On Bill Wiley Kellerman's Article

The Cleansing of the Temple – Sojourners (1980)

I love this article. It dates back to 1980—I would have been seventeen when he wrote it. Kellerman does a really good job drawing parallels between the kind of city Jerusalem was then and how we think about cities today, looking at it through the lens of systemic injustice. This was early work when that kind of analytical framework was coming into play.

Why We Understand More Now

Both this article and the more recent one by Corey Farr (2020) weave into their analysis our ability to access and work through archaeological records. A hundred years ago, making sense of a trove of documents meant spending months just documenting the list of them, then translating one or two. No time to synthesize the big picture.

It's only recently, with information technology, that we can pull it all together—much as we do for businesses today—to get a better sense of what life was like in Jesus' day.

On Risk and Martyrdom

Kellerman brings some very good balance to our discussion around risk and "taking up your cross." There's a danger—not just in Christianity but in the glorification of war—of addiction to risk. It can be attractive to someone who doesn't find purpose elsewhere: "Maybe my life is to take risk to the max on behalf of some great goal. What could be better than being a martyr for Christ?"

But the pursuit of martyrdom was declared a heresy. It's just not healthy. Kellerman's article is a lovely read that holds nonviolence, humility, and bold action together in proper tension.

Note: Sojourners gives you one of two free articles, so this one should be accessible.
